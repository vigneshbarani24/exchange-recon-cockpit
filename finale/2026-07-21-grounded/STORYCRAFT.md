# STORYCRAFT: the finale narrative, built on the week's own story

Source study: The AI Daily Brief (NLW), editions of 2026-07-13 through 2026-07-20, scraped
2026-07-22. Purpose: not imitation but alignment. Three of the seven editions studied carry
the exact argument this project makes, published days before the finale. The finale story
should stand on that ground: the discourse already turned our way; we are the demonstration.

Rules honored throughout: no em dashes in this file, never "three way match", no customer
names, write-back stays HELD, vocabulary lock from the red-team pass (live S/4HANA cloud
tenant; six-PO campaign, both gate branches; designed, parked, conservative by default;

## Part A: the technique playbook (what makes their storytelling work)

1. **Thesis-first.** One sentence reframes the entire topic before any detail. Pattern:
   "The real fight isn't A. It's B." Example (paraphrased from Jul 16): the enterprise
   fight is not which model you rent, it is who owns the one you customize.
   Apply: our reframe is "reasoning is real, authority is the problem."

2. **The misdirection-correction.** "It's the availability, not the benchmarks, that
   matters" (Jul 16 hook). Set up the expected axis, then swap it.
   Apply: "The demo question is usually how smart the agent is. The production question
   is what it is allowed to do."

3. **The aphorism hook.** Open cold on a sentence that could be a poster:
   "Autonomy without structure creates as much slop as leverage" (Jul 15, verbatim).
   No greeting, no agenda. Apply: our cold open already does this; keep it.

4. **The Take.** Every edition ends with an opinionated, time-stamped, actionable stance,
   never a summary. Apply: the finale close must be a stance ("AI is the new RPA, and it
   needs the same discipline RPA got"), never a recap.

5. **Quantified transformation as narrative beats.** "2.9x code per engineer, 60% faster
   hard-ticket closure" (Jul 19). Numbers carry the story; adjectives do not.
   Apply: 2 minutes 56 seconds end to end; 57 second verdict; 1.1 second tolerance check;
   twelve of twelve evals; six purchase orders; ten completed instances.

6. **The counterintuitive pairing.** "Output tripled while quality held" (Jul 19 pattern).
   Two facts the listener expects to trade off, delivered together.
   Apply: "the flow finishes in three minutes, and it will also wait forever. Both are
   the point."

7. **The human reframe.** "People don't feel like they've been automated. They feel like
   they've been promoted" (Jul 19, verbatim). Recast displacement as elevation.
   Apply: "the reconciler stops being a spreadsheet detective and becomes a signer of
   evidence-backed decisions."

8. **Concrete actors, kinetic verbs, shipping-now urgency.** "Apple goes thermonuclear on
   OpenAI" (Jul 13). Named systems, present tense, this week.
   Apply: name real objects on screen: purchase order 4500000021, item ten, RM27,
   twenty-five pounds. Never "an ERP", always "this purchase order."

9. **Question headlines create deliberation.** "Is Kimi K3 Really Fable Class?" invites
   the audience to judge before the reveal. Apply sparingly: one deliberate question to
   the jury before the peak: "how does the agent know that?"

10. **Slope, not y-intercept.** Their favorite trajectory frame (Jul 17, verbatim:
    "Look at the slope, not the y-intercept"). Apply to the roadmap: judge where this is
    pointed (Teams approval, write-back on the fixed server, Test Cloud regression), not
    just where it stands.

11. **Coined compounds become carriers.** "Self-driving company", "software factories",
    "inner loop / outer loop" (Jul 15, 19). A two-word coinage outlives the talk.
    Apply: ours is "the governed back office." Say it at least twice.

12. **Benchmarks versus vibes discipline.** Jul 17 contrasts curated demo excellence
    against real debugging failures. The audience is primed to distrust demos.
    Apply: this is why the evidence rail exists. Say "you do not have to take the demo's
    word for it" and point at instance IDs.

13. **Name the absence.** The Jul 16 edition, for all its sovereignty talk, never asks who
    is accountable when the model acts. The sharpest story move available to us is naming
    what the discourse skipped. Apply: Part C, line 1.

14. **Structural verification over promises.** Jul 15, on trust: verification must be
    structural, not promised. This is the field's own bar for governance claims.
    Apply: no write tool bound, a gate that suspends the instance, a bucket of evidence.
    Say "structural" out loud; it is their word for the thing we built.

15. **The zeitgeist pivot sentence.** Jul 15 marks the turn explicitly: last year the
    sentiment was unleash the agents; this year it is put the human back at the center.
    A story lands harder when it rides a turn the audience already feels.
    Apply: Part B, beat two is built on this.

## Part B: the five beats, rewritten in that voice

Timings and screen cues stay exactly as DUMP section 10. Words change; choreography does
not. Budgets at roughly 150 words per minute.

### Beat 1: cold open (0:00 to 0:20, ~50 words)
Screen: slide 1, then the invoice next to the SAP PO line.

> "A supplier invoiced us. Price and quantity do not match the purchase order in SAP.
> Someone has to work out why, against the live system of record, before money moves.
> Most agentic demos would now agree two numbers they made up. This one is about to read
> a purchase order it has never seen."

Unchanged in substance from DUMP; it already follows the aphorism-hook and
misdirection-correction patterns. Do not improve what is finished.

### Beat 2: the turn, and the argument (0:20 to 1:40, ~200 words)
Screen: start the instance at 0:20 and say so; slide 3, then the iceberg.

> "I am starting the process now. It takes about three minutes, so let me tell you why it
> is built this way while it runs.
>
> This year the field turned. Last year the advice was to unleash the agents. This year
> the people who actually run them are saying autonomy without structure creates as much
> slop as leverage, and the standout sentiment is: put the human back at the center. That
> turn is this build.
>
> The problem is old and expensive. A back office migrates, the transactions automate,
> and the exceptions stay human: five desks, four days, real money. An agent can be
> perfectly valid in the ERP and still be unauthorized in the business.
>
> So the architecture is a hierarchy of authority, not a pipeline. Below the waterline,
> determinism: a tolerance rule, two percent on price, one unit on quantity, one second,
> no model call. At the waterline, three exception-handling agents that reason only where
> determinism runs out. At the peak, a person: the only actor in this system who can
> commit anything. Reasoning is real. Authority is the problem. This is the governed
> back office."

### Beat 3: the gate, and the reveal (2:10 to 3:00, ~120 words)
Screen: switch to the instance, suspended at the gate; then the variance output. Slow down.

> "And there it is. The process has stopped. Three agents ran as real Orchestrator jobs
> inside this one instance, and now it is suspended, waiting for a person. It will wait
> as long as it takes. The industry is celebrating the self-driving company this month,
> and fair enough. But where the work is money in a system of record, the self-driving
> company still pulls over at the gate.
>
> Now the moment that decides whether you believe me. The agent was handed only the
> supplier's numbers. It is reporting the PO side: net price twenty-five pounds, order
> quantity five. Ask yourself how it knows that. There is exactly one way: it read
> S/4HANA at runtime."

### Beat 4: authority, held (3:00 to 4:15, ~180 words)
Screen: approve (per the DUMP branch note, from a visible portal surface, narrated as the
reviewer); posting-prep; the held correction; End_Corrected; then the build slide.

> "A person decides. I approve, and the process resumes.
>
> It builds the exact S/4 update: item ten, twenty-five to twenty-seven fifty. And then
> it stops. Prepared and held. The agents have no write tool at all; that is not a
> promise in a prompt, it is the tool contract. The only write path is a separate
> deterministic step a human authorizes. And the trust is structural, not promised:
> twelve of twelve eval cases pass, with the ground truth model-validated against the
> live tenant, and every run leaves its evidence in a bucket, reconstructable after the
> fact. Where the platform would not let the auto-clear branch fire, I filed the defect
> and made the conservative path the default: borderline cases go to the human.
>
> One more thing, because it changed the build: the agents, the BPMN, and the cockpit
> were built with Claude Code through UiPath for Coding Agents, and when the composition
> failed for two days, it was the coding agent that diagnosed the platform defect. The
> commit trail is public."

### Beat 5: the take (4:15 to 4:50, ~90 words)
Screen: slide 7, two value cards.

> "Two enterprises, one pattern. A refiner where this class of exception cost millions,
> and a UK adventure travel group taking two hundred supplier invoices a week through
> exactly this gate. Deployment is a configuration delta, not a rebuild.
>
> The industry spent this month arguing about who owns the model. The back office
> question is simpler and older: who owns the decision. In this process the answer is
> always a person, holding the pen, with the evidence to sign. AI is the new RPA, and it
> needs the same discipline RPA got. Thank you."

Hold 4:50 to 5:00. Do not fill it.

## Part C: zeitgeist positioning (build on the aligned episodes)

The three aligned editions and the move each one gives us:

1. **The absence to name (from Jul 16, "who owns the model").** That edition maps the
   entire sovereignty fight and never once asks who is accountable when the model acts.
   Ready line (used in beat 5): "The industry is arguing about who owns the model. The
   back office question is who owns the decision." If a judge raises model choice or
   vendor lock-in, this is the pivot: model access comes through the LLM Gateway, no raw
   keys in the repo, and the model is swappable precisely because authority never lives
   in the model.

2. **The foil to salute (from Jul 19, "the self-driving company").** The episode's own
   telling: agents drive "where work is verifiable," behind access policies and audit
   logging, escalating when human judgment is needed. Salute it, then add the missing
   clause. Ready line (used in beat 3): "where the work is money in a system of record,
   the self-driving company still pulls over at the gate." If asked about full autonomy:
   "their loop is goals, access, verifiable criteria. A financial correction has one more
   element: a signature. I kept it."

3. **The consensus to claim (from Jul 15, "5 AI engineering trends").** The field's own
   words this month: autonomy without structure creates slop; put the human back at the
   center; trust must be structural, not promised. Ready line (used in beats 2 and 4).
   In Q&A, if either judge frames governance as friction: "the engineering community
   already voted on this. Structure is not the tax on autonomy, it is what makes the
   leverage real."

Echo, do not cite. The judges live in this discourse; hearing its language mapped onto a
running SAP process is the effect. Naming the podcast on stage spends seconds and adds
nothing; if it ever helps, the shape is "the sentiment across the field this month," never
a source citation mid-demo.
